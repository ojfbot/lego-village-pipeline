The drafting table on fixtures — built bag by bag

Drawn 2026-09-18 · gate 2026-10-21 · 33 days

Ten Claude Design prototypes become one running app on typed fixtures. The fixtures pass the same validators real data will pass, so the mock UI is the schema gate. Six numbered bags, each with its own booklet and the picture on the box agreed before it opens. Five agents and one operator work in lanes; design cuts every Friday; a pin, a drift report and a change request keep design and build in step. Frame watches from the tooling plane.

1 How a bag is built — learning through play

The shape of building a LEGO set: open the numbered bag, follow the booklet, look at the model while you build, compare with the picture on the box, step back. The LEGO Foundation's five characteristics — joyful, meaningful, actively engaged, iterative, socially interactive — are why it looks this way.

2 The calendar, with lanes

One implementer in the build lane; design cuts every Friday; review beside. Bags 4–6 overlap on purpose. If it slips, P-01 optimisation is sacrificed first; the six fit checks, M-01 and the C-01 rows with chips do not slip — they are what the gate is for.

3 How design keeps moving while a bag builds

A bag builds against one pinned package revision. Design never edits that revision; it ships the next one as Friday's cut, seeded from the same fixtures the app runs on. Every cut is diffed, and every diff on a sheet in build is triaged before it touches code. A sheet has one owner at a time.

4 What gets built, and the one seam

Packages read downward. The only place mock and real differ is the DataSource adapter; the sheets cannot tell which one is plugged in. Frame touches the tooling plane only.
