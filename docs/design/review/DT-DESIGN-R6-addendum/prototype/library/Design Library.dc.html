Radius grammar — the brick and the stud. Cards keep three molded fillets and one stud corner (--dt-r-card); controls a plain fillet (--dt-r-btn); perfect circles (--dt-r-pill) reserved for gauges and studs. Shadows are the vapor pair: terracotta ghost + blue echo.
⤢
Card behaviour — grow with content to a cap, then scroll; expand on demand. A card sizes to its children up to a per-card cap (max-height, eased), then scrolls internally; it never pushes the grid past that cap. Every card carries a ⤢ toggle: expanded, it spans the full row and takes up to min(72vh, 760px) while its neighbours collapse to their title rows (72px) until it is restored. One card expanded at a time. First pass in Prototype C; the full grid choreography (animated row/column reflow) is a dedicated design-system session — OPEN.
A2b · HATCH — GHOSTED DRAFTING FILLS · EXPERIMENT 09-18
Five hatch fills, all in dt/tokens.css as --dt-hatch-*. They are fill-only tokens — they make no contrast claim of their own, so the contract is one rule: hatch always moves the surface away from the text colour. On paper it darkens toward ink; on blueprint it darkens below the sheet, which raises contrast for light ink. That asymmetry is why blueprint hatch may run three times denser than paper hatch at the same visual weight. Nothing here lightens a surface — a lightening hatch on blueprint would take --dt-muted from 5.5:1 to about 2.5:1 in one step.
--DT-HATCH-GHOST · 4% Body text sits on this. Barely there — use for a whole card that is merely proposed. ■ TEXT-SAFE · MUTED 5.2:1
--DT-HATCH-DRAFT · 7%+4% Two periods (7px / 13px) at two line widths — the sketchy read comes from the beat between them, not from darkness. ■ TEXT-SAFE · MUTED 4.9:1
--DT-HATCH-CROSS · 7%+5%+3% Cross-hatch, deliberately unequal angles so the grid never resolves. This is the ceiling for text. ◇ TEXT-SAFE AT THE LIMIT · MUTED 4.7:1
--DT-HATCH-DENSE · 11%+9% Too dark for text. Any copy over dense hatch sits on an opaque plate like this one — the plate is --dt-sheet, so the measured contrast is the ordinary token contrast. ▲ TEXT-FREE OR PLATE ONLY · BARE 4.1:1
--DT-HATCH-TINT · ACCENT 8% The one coloured hatch, drawn from --dt-accent-deep. Reserve it for one meaning per sheet — "held back" reads best. ◇ TEXT-SAFE · NEVER TWO TINTS ON ONE SHEET
RULES BEFORE THIS LEAVES THE EXPERIMENT
1 · Hatch is ornament, not notation. It is never legended, never explained, and never the thing a reader is asked to decode — the explicit label, chip or sentence carries the meaning. Hatch varies with state so the surface feels different before anyone reads anything; association builds by repetition across sheets, the way ornament has always worked.
2 · Baked in as --dt-hatch-card. Every sheet card in the deck — Hub, A-01, B-01, C-01, I-01, M-01, P-01, F-01, J-01, H-01, this library — now carries background-image:var(--dt-hatch-card) (ghost) as its default texture. State-bearing cards escalate from there; nothing in the deck is flat paper any more.
2b · Background-image only, never background. Set background-image:var(--dt-hatch-draft) and leave background-color:var(--dt-sheet) alone, so the opaque base under the strokes is still the token surface.
3 · The cap is set by --dt-muted, not by ink. Ink on sheet has ~13:1 to spend; muted has 5.3:1. Peak alpha where strokes cross is held to 12% on paper for that reason. New densities get checked against muted, never against ink.
4 · Losing it costs nothing. Because no meaning rides on the pattern, a reader who never perceives it misses nothing — prefers-contrast:more and forced-colors set all five tokens to none.
5 · Not under inputs, chips, or the canvas. Editable fields and small status chips keep flat surfaces; hatch lives on card and band backgrounds where a stroke crossing a glyph stem is still 1px of 12%.
Settled 09-18 (operator): every state is hatched, no flat card — ghost for built and standing, cross for in pieces and free to raid, tint for pieces held back, draft for not in the house yet. No legend anywhere: the fills are too faint to be read as a key, which is exactly the point. They work at the intuitive level while the words do the telling.

C2 · GAPCLOSER — TURN A STRIKE INTO QUEUED WORK
What it is. A chip that sits on any option, request or row carrying open strikes (○ NOT MODELED · CAN’T RUN YET · NO PARTS LIST · NO PRICES · FIT UNPROVEN). Tap it and you get the work list that would close them: what each job does in plain words, which runner does it (Blender agent · geometry check · Studio agent · research agent · fit check), what it gives back, and what it must wait for.
What it never does. Choose for you. Results land as evidence on the option — with method and hash — and the option stays an option. AI stance is never authority (014 §8).
States. Chip: ◇ n STRIKES · CLOSE THEM → ◌ n RUNNING · m OPEN → ■ GAPS CLOSED. Row: ○ OPEN · ◇ QUEUED · ◌ RUNNING · ■ CLOSED · ✎ BY HAND. Queuing one job pulls in what it waits for. “Queue all, in order” respects the dependency order. Restate before run: “I’ll queue 5 jobs in this order … about 52 minutes … nothing gets chosen for you” → Yes, run it / Not yet.
By hand. Any job can be marked ✎ BY HAND — the operator will do it and attach the file later. Honest provenance for a manual step beats a guessed one (014 §3.4).
Accessibility. Chip is a button with aria-expanded; panel is a non-modal dialog; focus moves to the first action on open and back to the chip on close; Esc closes; each QUEUE / BY HAND has a full-sentence name; the restatement is role=status aria-live=assertive; per-row progress notes are polite live regions.
Used by. C-01 (double ballast) today; A-01 planting proposals, P-01 “unpriced” rows, F-01 “not modeled yet” stickers, M-01 unverified measurements next. Props: subject, gaps[] (id · label · strike · how · runner · produces · after[] · mins), onStatus(status, id, to) — the host sheet reads this and re-derives its own strikes, stances, asks and blockers (C-01 does: DOUBLE flips to ■ MODELED / ■ FIT as jobs close). Schema request: a WorkItem per queued job, joined to the option it closes and to the evidence it returns (see 014 §7.6 envelope).

C3 · SETREF — EVERY SET, MOC OR PART NUMBER IS A REFERENCE CARD
What it is. An inline reference: the number stays readable text with a dotted underline; hover or focus opens a card — image, kind + number, name, the facts we hold about it, BrickLink and Rebrickable links. Reference data comes from BrickLink (catalog, inventory, price guide), never LEGO.com. For a Studio MOC the card points at the stable gallery URL (design.page?idModel=…), names the creator verbatim with the rights we hold, and links our own hashed snapshot from B-01 — the listing can vanish; the snapshot cannot.
Rules. Card is role=tooltip, tied to the link by aria-describedby; opens on hover and keyboard focus; Esc closes. One card page-wide — opening another closes the current one. Timing: 120 ms intent delay → 140 ms fade in; 180 ms grace → 150 ms fade out. Painted into a body-level layer so no sheet stacking context can cover it. The lifecycle lives once, in dt/overlay.js — SetRef and CrossRef both attach to it, so “one card page-wide” holds across kinds (GapCloser to follow, OW-02). Fully opaque sheet background — never translucent. The image slot is fillable once and persists. Unknown numbers show an honest “no reference cached yet”.
Used by. M-01 (tree rows), C-01 (sources). Next: A-01 rig rows, P-01 part rows, F-01 “Tree” (kid-safe copy variant), PartPopover merges into this for parts. Props: num · kind (set|moc|part) · label · name? · facts?. Schema: Asset { vendor, product_url } already requested — add image_ref, fetched_at.

C4 · CROSSREF + LANDING — LINKS BETWEEN SHEETS PREVIEW, THEN FLASH WHERE THEY LAND
What it is. Any link from one sheet to a card on another is a CrossRef chip: hover or focus shows a preview of the target card — sheet · card id · its current status chip · title · one-line summary — so you know what you’ll find before you go. Click navigates to sheet#card.
Landing. Every sheet loads dt/landing.js. On arrival with a #card hash it scrolls the card to the upper third and fires the token-level dtLand flash (1.6 s accent ring that swells and fades) on the whole card, and announces “Landed on: {card title}” politely. Ids live on card containers, not titles.
Direction. dir=back (↖ “where it came from”) and forward (→ “what leans on it”) share one component; the preview header says which.
Honesty. Previews read window.dtCards, a registry each sheet fills; today it is seeded in landing.js (MOCK). A real host reads the manifest. Unregistered targets say so instead of inventing a summary.
Used by. M-01 claims (back + forward) and measurement rows (forward). Next: A-01 fit checks → M-01, C-01 asks → M-01 / P-01, Hub decision blast-radius chips. Same overlay rule as SetRef: body layer, opaque, one open at a time, 120/140 in · 180/150 out.

C5 · RELEASEGATE — A FORMAL GATE AS A QUIET CARD AND A FRIENDLY STAMP
What it is. The 014 §8 formal gate — baseline release, procurement release, rights pass — as one component. Left: what’s being released (subject + revision, CrossRef back) and evidence it rests on (badge · text · freshness · CrossRef). Right: open findings, each with severity and three calls: fix (CrossRef to the closing sheet), waive (a reason sentence on the record), defer (owner + due trigger). Then initials + Release / Return for work. HIGH findings block; the label becomes “· with waivers” when any are waived or deferred. The §8 record sits behind a disclosure; the stamp is what you see.
Rules. Lives at the end of the sheet that produces the subject (C-01 for procurement release — DEC-034), never at the head of the consumer. The consumer (P-01) only reads the result. AI stance is analysis, never authority. Family surfaces never see a gate (K-7): F-01’s send is a proposal. Props: question · subject* · evidence[] · findings[] · approveLabel · returnLabel · gateId · gateType · onChange(kind: passed | reopened | returned | finding). Announcements: the tag is a polite live region; approve-disabled reason is written beside the button.

C6 · BLOCKER — “BLOCKED BY” IS NEVER JUST A LABEL
Rule (operator, 2026-09-17). Wherever a step says it is blocked, each blocker is a chip: hover or focus previews the card that owns it (where · status · title · why); the popover carries the action that clears it when one exists — same effect as the button on the card, recorded the same way — and a link that lands (with the flash) when it must be cleared where it lives. Click pins the popover open; Esc closes.
Props. label · sheet · card · title · summary · status · actionLabel · actionNote · onAction? · linkLabel. Built on dt/overlay.js like SetRef and CrossRef (one open page-wide). Card is role=dialog named “Blocker: …”.
Used by. C-01 approval trail (open request → park · recolours → derive · fit → run · double-ballast → GapCloser link · LH’s OK → request card). Next: ReleaseGate findings, A-01 “Does it fit?” blocks, P-01 revalidation.
